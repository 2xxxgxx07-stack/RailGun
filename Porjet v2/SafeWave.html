Crafting Digital Experiences That
SafeWave

SafeWave is a real-time disaster monitoring and early warning platform. It aggregates multi-source data, assesses threats via AI, and delivers geo-targeted emergency alerts to help citizens and authorities prepare and respond effectively to natural and man-made disasters.

Prototype View Information

Overview

SafeWave is a disaster monitoring and early warning platform designed to aggregate real-time data from multiple sources and deliver targeted alerts to citizens and emergency authorities. The platform uses AI-driven threat assessment to filter and prioritize genuine hazards, reducing the problem of alert fatigue from generic mass notifications. The project focuses on providing accurate, location-specific warnings that enable effective preparation and response before disasters escalate.

UX Process

The design process began with research into how citizens and authorities currently receive disaster information and what prevents effective early response. Research revealed that information overload, unverified sources, and generic alerts were the primary barriers to public preparedness. These insights informed the platform's information architecture, alert delivery logic, and monitoring dashboard design. Wireframes and prototypes were tested with both public users and emergency management professionals to validate clarity and usability.

Solution & Concept

SafeWave centralizes disaster monitoring data from verified sources and uses AI to assess threat levels before delivering geo-targeted alerts. Citizens receive relevant warnings based on their location and the specific hazard type, reducing unnecessary alerts and improving trust in the system. Authorities access a real-time monitoring dashboard with multi-source data aggregation, threat mapping, and communication tools. The concept focuses on Verified Information, Geo-Targeted Alerts, Reduced Alert Fatigue, and Coordinated Response.

Design thinking

Empathize

Research explored how citizens respond to disaster warnings and how emergency management authorities monitor and communicate threats. Citizens reported confusion from conflicting information across social media, news, and official channels, and frustration with receiving alerts that were not relevant to their location. Emergency authorities described the challenge of consolidating real-time data from multiple monitoring systems during active events. User Personas were developed for general public users and emergency management professionals. Empathy Maps captured the fear, uncertainty, and urgency experienced during disaster events and the need for trusted, immediate, and actionable information.

Define

Key challenges were defined around three areas: unverified and scattered disaster information leaving the public confused, generic mass alerts causing warning fatigue and reducing public trust, and emergency authorities lacking a single integrated monitoring view. Problem Statements and Design Goals were established to guide the solution toward a platform that verifies information before alerting, personalizes warnings by location and hazard type, and provides authorities with real-time situational awareness. User Journey Maps traced both the citizen alert experience and the authority monitoring workflow during a disaster event.

Ideate

Ideation explored how to present complex real-time monitoring data in a way that supports fast, confident decision-making for both citizens and authorities. User Flows mapped the citizen alert notification journey and the authority monitoring workflow from threat detection to public communication. A Sitemap organized the platform into a citizen-facing alert and preparedness interface and an authority-facing monitoring and communication dashboard. Key design decisions focused on how to display threat severity, geographic scope, and recommended actions clearly for users with varying levels of emergency awareness.

Prototype

Wireframes established the structure of the citizen alert notification screen, the threat map view, the preparedness information hub, and the authority monitoring dashboard. High-fidelity designs applied a bold, high-contrast visual system with clear severity color coding and intuitive map-based data visualization. An interactive prototype simulated a disaster alert scenario — from threat detection and AI assessment to citizen notification and authority coordination — for testing across both user groups.

Test

Usability testing was conducted with general public users and emergency management professionals using simulated disaster scenarios. Citizens evaluated how quickly they could understand the nature of an alert, determine its relevance to their location, and identify the recommended action. Authorities tested how effectively the monitoring dashboard supported situational awareness and communication during a multi-hazard event. Feedback drove improvements to alert clarity, map readability, and the information hierarchy of the citizen-facing interface.

Testing Result & Feedback

  • Higher Alert Relevance — Geo-targeted notifications significantly improved perceived relevance compared to generic mass alerts.
  • Faster Threat Comprehension — Users identified threat severity and recommended actions more quickly with the visual severity system.
  • Improved Authority Coordination — The centralized dashboard gave emergency managers better situational awareness during multi-hazard scenarios.
  • Increased Public Trust — Verified information sourcing and clear alert explanations improved user confidence in the system.

Design System

Design System

The SafeWave Design System uses bold colors, high contrast, and clear semantic severity indicators to ensure that critical information is immediately understood. It includes map components, alert cards, severity color tokens, notification patterns, and data visualization guidelines for real-time monitoring dashboards.

Mood and tone

SafeWave's visual direction is bold, authoritative, and clear. The interface uses strong contrast and a dark base with vivid alert colors to communicate severity and urgency without ambiguity. The design balances the serious nature of disaster communication with an approachable interface that keeps users calm and focused on the most important information and actions.
