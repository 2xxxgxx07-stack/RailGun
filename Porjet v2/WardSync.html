WardSync

WardSync is an Inpatient Ward Management System designed to support collaboration between doctors, nurses, and pharmacists. It centralizes patient information, treatment plans, medical orders, and nursing records in one platform, helping healthcare teams manage patient care more efficiently and seamlessly.

Protype View Information

Overview

WardSync is a hospital ward management system designed to connect healthcare professionals and simplify the clinical workflow. The system supports nurses, doctors, and pharmacists in accessing patient information, managing clinical tasks, reviewing medical orders, and coordinating patient care. The project focuses on reducing fragmented information and unnecessary workflow steps while improving information visibility and collaboration across different healthcare roles. Through a user-centered design approach, WardSync aims to create a more efficient, clear, and safer experience for healthcare professionals.

UX Process

The project followed a Design Thinking approach to understand users, define key problems, explore solutions, and validate the design. I conducted research to understand the workflows and challenges of nurses, doctors, and pharmacists. These insights were translated into personas, empathy maps, problem statements, and user journey maps. I then explored the information architecture, sitemap, and user flows before developing wireframes and high-fidelity prototypes. Usability testing and user feedback were used to identify usability issues and refine the final solution.

Solution & Concept

The concept of WardSync is built around a Unified Clinical Workflow, bringing critical patient information and related tasks into a more connected experience. Instead of requiring users to navigate through multiple areas of the system, relevant information is organized around the patient's care journey. Doctors can review clinical information and create orders, pharmacists can verify medication and allergy information, while nurses can receive orders, complete care tasks, and document patient information. The solution focuses on Centralized Information, Simplified Workflow, Clear Status, and Cross-Team Collaboration.

Design thinking

Empathize

To better understand the challenges of healthcare professionals, I started by studying the daily workflows of nurses, doctors, and pharmacists. Through user research, I explored how each role accesses patient information, manages clinical tasks, and collaborates with other teams. The research revealed that each role has different priorities but relies on the same patient information throughout the care process. Nurses focus on patient care and documentation, doctors on clinical decisions, and pharmacists on medication safety. I used User Personas to represent their goals, behaviors, and challenges. Empathy Maps were then created to understand what users say, think, do, and feel during their workflow. These insights became the foundation for identifying key problems and opportunities for WardSync.

Define

Based on the research findings, I identified several challenges across the clinical workflow, particularly around information access, task management, and collaboration. Nurses needed to manage large amounts of patient information while completing tasks and handovers. Doctors needed to quickly review clinical data from multiple sources before making treatment decisions. Pharmacists needed to verify medication orders and allergy information before dispensing medication. These challenges highlighted a common issue: critical information was distributed across different parts of the workflow. I used Problem Statements to clearly define the challenges faced by each user role. Design Goals and User Journey Maps were then created to identify opportunities for improving the overall clinical experience.

Ideate

After defining the key problems, I explored different ways to simplify the clinical workflow and improve collaboration between healthcare professionals. I focused on creating a more connected experience where users could access relevant patient information without unnecessary navigation. User Flows were created to map the key actions and interactions for each role, from accessing patient information to completing clinical tasks. I also developed a Sitemap to organize the system's information architecture and ensure that features were grouped logically. The structure was designed around real user tasks rather than simply organizing features by system functions. This helped establish a clear foundation for the interface and ensured that the proposed solution could support different clinical workflows.

Prototype

I translated the proposed workflows and information architecture into interface concepts through an iterative prototyping process. I started with Low-Fidelity Wireframes to explore layout, content hierarchy, navigation, and task flows without focusing heavily on visual details. After reviewing the structure, I developed High-Fidelity Designs that incorporated the visual design system, components, information hierarchy, and interaction patterns. Key screens were designed around the most important tasks for nurses, doctors, and pharmacists. The prototype connected these screens into realistic workflows to simulate how users would interact with WardSync in their daily work. This allowed the design to be evaluated and refined before moving toward the final solution.

Test

The prototype was evaluated through usability testing to understand how effectively users could complete their key tasks. Testing focused on common clinical scenarios such as reviewing patient information, checking clinical results, managing medication orders, and completing nursing tasks. Users were asked to perform tasks using the prototype while their interactions, difficulties, and feedback were observed. The testing helped identify areas where information was difficult to find, actions were unclear, or workflows required unnecessary steps. I used these findings to prioritize usability issues based on their impact on the user's workflow. The design was then iterated based on the feedback to improve clarity, efficiency, and overall usability.

Testing Result & Feedback

  • Improved Information Discoverability — Users were able to locate key patient information more easily through a centralized patient overview.
  • Clearer Clinical Workflow — The redesigned workflow helped users understand what actions needed to be completed at each stage.
  • Better Information Hierarchy — Important information such as Vital Signs, Lab Results, Medication Orders, and Allergy History became easier to identify.
  • More Visible Primary Actions — Key actions such as reviewing orders, confirming medication, and updating patient information were easier to discover.

Design System

Design System

The WardSync Design System was created to maintain consistency, scalability, and usability across the application. It includes foundational elements such as color, typography, spacing, icons, and grid systems, as well as reusable UI components for common clinical workflows. Components such as buttons, input fields, cards, tables, navigation, status indicators, alerts, and patient information modules were designed with different states and use cases in mind. The system helps maintain a consistent visual language across different user roles while allowing the interface to scale as new features and workflows are introduced.

Mood and tone

The visual direction of WardSync focuses on creating an interface that feels professional, trustworthy, calm, and easy to understand. Since the system is used in a healthcare environment where users work under time pressure, the design avoids unnecessary visual complexity and emphasizes clarity and information hierarchy. A clean layout, balanced spacing, subtle visual elements, and clear status indicators help users quickly identify important information and actions.
